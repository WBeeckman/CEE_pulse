Brent surged to roughly $85–86/bbl — the highest since mid-June, up ~11% in two sessions and about 40% year-to-date. ING's commodities desk now says the June memorandum of understanding "is starting to look well and truly dead." Trump told Fox News the US may strike power plants and bridges next week unless Tehran negotiates; he also dropped a proposed 20% Hormuz transit fee after the IMO called mandatory tolls illegal. Strait traffic has collapsed to a trickle (57 transits Fri–Sun versus ~130 vessels a day pre-war). Direct CEE exposure: Europe draws 12–14% of its LNG from Qatar through the strait, and the Gulf supplies up to 30% of globally traded fertilizer — so this now threatens energy, food and industrial input costs at once, hitting import-dependent Romania, Slovakia and Bulgaria hardest. Every disinflation forecast on this page predates it.

⚠ Volkswagen's "Zielbild 2030" — the biggest single risk to CEE industry: VW's supervisory board met on 9 July on CEO Oliver Blume's restructuring plan: up to 100,000 jobs globally (~15% of the workforce), four German plants reportedly at risk (Hannover, Emden, Zwickau, Audi Neckarsulm), half the model range cut, targeting €15bn in annual savings. Roughly 50,000 cuts are already agreed across VW, Audi, Porsche and Cariad; Blume's internal note says group costs are still ~20% above rivals, and closing that gap could mean 50,000 more. Drivers: Chinese competition, tariffs and expensive German production. Nothing is final — VW hasn't officially confirmed, German unions and Lower Saxony (~20% of voting rights) are resisting, and management is still assessing "brands, companies and regions." Why CEE cares: automotive is the region's industrial backbone. The exposure is uneven — Slovakia looks most at risk, Czechia says it's insulated, Poland may even gain (see fact-check).

Political decisions with economic impact — developments in the last 30 days

🇨🇿 Czechia

Czech divisions went public at the 7–8 July NATO Ankara summit: President Pavel and PM Babiš both attended after months of dispute over who should lead the delegation, arriving on separate planes about an hour apart and standing at opposite ends of the group photo. Separately, Babiš announced a CZK 36bn defence-budget increase lifting spending to 2% of GDP, and said Czechia won't block the EU's Ukraine-aid package but won't contribute financially, citing the NATO target.

🇵🇱 Poland

A public-hospital scandal has dominated Polish politics for weeks: a 28-year-old junior doctor and Civic Coalition councillor allegedly earned ~1.6m zloty (€375k) in 2025 running an ER without the required qualification. The Health Minister responded with plans to cap hospital hourly rates and medical salaries and tighten oversight — a politically charged intervention in health spending. Meanwhile President Nawrocki met Zelensky at the Ankara NATO summit, thawing a memory-politics row, even as his record run of vetoes continues to block the law needed for Poland's €44bn SAFE defence-loan access.

🇭🇺 Hungary

With EU funds now flowing after May's unlock, the political focus has shifted to fiscal credibility: PM Magyar said the 2026 deficit will most likely exceed 7% of GDP (over 8% counting the funds), and Finance Minister András Kármán announced Hungary will overhaul its budget process to restore credibility after years of slippage — while also setting a goal of meeting the Maastricht criteria by 2030 to open a path to euro adoption.
Erste / FXStreet — 6 Jul 2026 · Read source

🇷🇴 Romania

Romania's political vacuum deepened: on 8 July the Bucharest Tribunal suspended all resolutions of the PNL's Extraordinary Congress — its new statute, new leadership, and its decision to rule out a PSD coalition. Interim PM Ilie Bolojan, whose coalition fell to a May no-confidence vote, governs with limited powers; President Nicușor Dan has ruled out early elections for now. The BNR explicitly cited domestic political uncertainty as a factor pressuring the leu and bond yields, and the deadlock is delaying reforms needed to unlock ~€10bn in EU recovery funds before an August deadline.
G4Media / BNR / Romania Insider — 6–8 Jul 2026 · Original (RO) · English

🇧🇬 Bulgaria

Radev's government, fresh off April's majority that ended a five-year crisis, has run straight into Brussels: on 10 July the Council of the EU formally opened an Excessive Deficit Procedure against Bulgaria — six months after euro entry — demanding corrective measures by 15 October. It lands as the government tries to pass its first budget, with opposition MPs (Democratic Bulgaria's Martin Dimitrov, GERB-SDS's Denitsa Sacheva) warning the 5.7% draft deficit points to future tax rises and higher labour costs. The procedure risks feeding the Euroscepticism that the changeover already stirred.

🇸🇰 Slovakia

A nationwide referendum on 4 July — to end PM Fico's lifetime allowance and restore the anti-corruption Special Prosecutor's Office and National Crime Agency — was declared invalid on turnout, with just 16.1% voting (a majority of the 4.3m electorate was required). Of those who did vote, ~93% backed scrapping Fico's allowance and ~92% backed restoring the two anti-corruption bodies — a symbolic rebuke that changes nothing legally, leaving the government's consolidation drive and EU-friction foreign policy intact.

⚖ Fact-check — conflicting accounts

Where sources give genuinely different accounts of the same event. Each entry lays out the competing versions side by side, states what can actually be established, and dates the assessment — rather than collapsing the disagreement into a single narrative. Conflicts cluster in Romania and Bulgaria, where English-language wires lag the local press.

Unresolved Is the Strait of Hormuz open or closed? assessed 14 Jul
Tehran's claim
After renewed strikes on 12–13 July, Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz "closed until further notice" — the chokepoint for roughly a fifth of the world's seaborne oil. Bloomberg
Washington's denial
US Central Command flatly denied the closure, saying it had launched further attacks specifically to keep the waterway open and ensure freedom of navigation. Bloomberg
Where it stands: Directly contradictory claims, and the picture has worsened: the US has now reimposed its naval blockade, Brent is at ~$85–86, and ING says the June MoU looks "well and truly dead." Ship-tracking shows transits collapsing to a trickle regardless of who is right — which is what actually matters for prices. For CEE this is the single biggest swing factor for the inflation path; every disinflation forecast on this page predates it.
Disputed exposure Do VW's 100,000 job cuts actually hit CEE — or not? assessed 15 Jul
"CEE is insulated"
Škoda says it expects no direct impact, with no job or production changes and full capacity. VW Slovakia has announced no layoffs or investment cuts, and its collective agreement runs to end-2027. Slovak outlets note the reported cuts concern German plants specifically, and Bratislava builds premium EVs with high added value. Global Banking & Finance
"Bratislava is in danger"
Pravda ran the headline "four German factories to close — Bratislava is also in danger!" German media report VW is weighing shifting Porsche Cayenne output to Leipzig. Blume's own note says management is assessing "brands, companies and regions," and Techbyte concludes it is not yet possible to say whether Bratislava is hit. Techbyte (SK)
Where it stands: Both sides are quoting real facts, and neither is settled — the plan itself isn't final and VW hasn't officially confirmed it. The important distinction: "no cuts announced" is not "no cuts coming", and the corporate reassurances are the companies' own positions, not independent findings. Note the three CEE countries face genuinely different exposures rather than one shared fate — Slovakia most at risk (largest foreign plant, Cayenne relocation talk), Czechia claiming insulation, Poland possibly gaining as German costs bite. Watch the Cayenne decision as the concrete test.
Disputed framing Hungary's EU-funds "unlock": clean win, or conditional and partial? assessed 14 Jul
The government's framing
PM Magyar called it a "historic day"; ~€16.4bn unblocked (€10bn Recovery Facility + €4.2bn + €2.2bn cohesion), about 13% of the budget, after reforms delivered "in record time." Euronews
The fine print
The release was tied to specific rule-of-law reforms, and Hungary still had until end-August to submit a plan to actually draw the recovery money. Magyar himself said the 2026 deficit would exceed 7% of GDP — over 8% with the funds. Al Jazeera
Where it stands: Both are true. A genuine reversal from the Orbán-era freeze — but "unlocked" describes a conditional process still mid-flight, not a completed transfer, and it does not fix the underlying deficit.
Disputed cause Did euro adoption drive Bulgaria's spring inflation spike? assessed 14 Jul
Popular perception
Widespread fear that the 1 January changeover would push prices up; dual lev/euro pricing was mandated through August and anti-corruption protests had toppled a government weeks earlier. Food prices ran hot into the switch. Al Jazeera
The ECB / official read
The changeover's direct price effect was "limited and largely one-off," concentrated in services; the larger spring rise toward ~6% was energy-driven and regional, not a euro effect. ECB blog
Where it stands: Evidence favours the official read on causation — the euro added a small one-off, energy did the heavy lifting — but the perception is politically real, and both framings keep running side by side in coverage.
Analyst split After the oil shock, which way do CEE currencies and rates go? assessed 14 Jul
Medium-term divergence view
ING and VT Markets: bullish CZK and HUF, bearish PLN — Czech/Hungarian banks react more decisively to inflation, while the zloty is capped by fiscal risk and less favourable carry. ING via FXStreet
Near-term pressure view
Soft June prints (CZ 1.5%, HU 1.7%) plus a dovish NBP could weigh on CEE FX now; and the oil-shock reversal reopens the inflation risk that could force central banks to hold rather than cut. ING via FXStreet
Where it stands: Partly resolved as of mid-July — the divergence call is playing out: the zloty has slid to an 18-month low (~4.35) on the dovish NBP while forint and koruna held firm, exactly as the divergence view predicted. What's still open is the oil path: renewed Hormuz risk could yet force the whole region's central banks to hold rather than cut, overriding the country-by-country story.
