Political decisions with economic impact — last 30 days

🇨🇿 Czechia

PM Babiš announced a CZK 36bn increase to the defense budget, pushing spending to 2% of GDP for the first time under this government — timed to the NATO summit in Ankara. Czechia will not block the EU's proposed €70bn Ukraine aid package but will not contribute financially, saying the funds are needed to hit the NATO target.

🇵🇱 Poland

President Nawrocki issued a record 37th veto — more than any Polish president in history — continuing to block government legislation, including the law needed to access Poland's €44bn share of the EU's SAFE defense-loan program. Tusk's government is proceeding with defense spending anyway via workarounds, but more flexible uses of the funds (police, border guard modernization) remain blocked.
Notes from Poland — 12 Jun 2026 · Read source

🇭🇺 Hungary

The EU moved to release over €16bn in previously frozen funds after PM Péter Magyar's government delivered on anti-corruption and rule-of-law pledges — a major reversal after years of Hungarian funds being withheld under the previous administration. The forint has rallied on the news.
FocusEconomics — early Jun 2026 · Read source

🇷🇴 Romania

Romania remains without a fully empowered government: interim PM Ilie Bolojan — whose coalition collapsed in a no-confidence vote in May — is expected to stay on with limited powers until at least August as coalition talks stall. President Nicușor Dan has ruled out early elections for now, but the political vacuum is delaying the fiscal reforms needed to unlock ~€10bn in EU recovery funds before an August deadline.
Romania Insider — 6 Jul 2026 · Read source

🇧🇬 Bulgaria

Rumen Radev's Progressive Bulgaria, which won an outright majority in April — ending a five-year political crisis and eight elections — is now moving to raise the debt ceiling for EU-funded projects by €3.8bn, after repeated snap elections delayed adoption of the 2026 budget and slowed infrastructure and energy-transition spending.
PolitPro — late Jun 2026 · Read source

🇸🇰 Slovakia

PM Fico's government continues its fiscal consolidation drive — tax and social-contribution increases took effect at the start of 2026 — while maintaining a foreign-policy posture (opposing EU sanctions on Russia, resisting using frozen Russian assets for Ukraine) that keeps Slovakia at odds with most of the bloc. Slovakia resumed receiving Russian crude via the Druzhba pipeline on 23 April after a months-long dispute.
